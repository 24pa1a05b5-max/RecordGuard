# RecordGuard AI — Limitations & Responsible Use

## 1. Regulatory and Demonstration Status

RecordGuard AI is a software prototype developed for hackathon demonstration purposes under problem statement **PS-11R3**.

- **Not a Certified Medical Device**: RecordGuard AI has not been reviewed, cleared, or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), the European Medicines Agency (EMA), or any other regulatory health authority.
- **Not for Diagnostic or Treatment Decisions**: This software must **never** be used as the sole basis for clinical diagnosis, prescribing, dosage modification, surgery, or treatment decisions.
- **Demonstration Data**: All default cohorts, documents, and evaluation datasets consist exclusively of synthetically constructed medical scenarios. No real Protected Health Information (PHI) is present in the repository.

---

## 2. Technical Limitations

1. **Synthetic Training Distribution**:
   - The ML classifier was fitted on 3,200 synthetic statement pairs generated by deterministic clinical templates.
   - While the model achieves high accuracy on these synthetic patterns, real-world clinical narrative text exhibits unstructured idiosyncrasies, informal shorthand, dictation typos, and provider-specific documentation habits that may reduce out-of-distribution performance.

2. **OCR Dependency**:
   - Document extraction relies on `pymupdf` for selectable PDF text and falls back to Tesseract OCR when available.
   - When scanned documents have low DPI, handwritten physician notes, or skewed orientations, OCR accuracy degrades. RecordGuard explicitly reports unreadable text rather than fabricating citations.

3. **Incomplete Record Context**:
   - RecordGuard evaluates pairwise statement evidence. If a relevant clinical fact (such as a medication allergy test conducted elsewhere) is absent from the provided documents, the system cannot deduce it.
   - Omission of an allergy or medication is explicitly classified as `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, because the absence of documentation is not clinical proof of absence.

4. **Deterministic Normalization Scope**:
   - The current dictionary and regular-expression normalization rules cover high-prevalence medications (e.g., Metformin, Lisinopril, Levothyroxine), allergies (Penicillin, Sulfa, Aspirin), common diagnoses (Diabetes, Hypertension, AFib), and standard lab panels (HbA1c, Glucose, Creatinine).
   - Rare diagnoses or novel biopharmaceuticals not present in the synonym dictionaries require professional manual review.

---

## 3. Responsible AI Principles in Clinical Informatics

- **Human-in-the-Loop**: The software never automatically overwrites or alters an electronic health record. All discrepancy classifications serve as advisory notifications requiring authorized clinician review.
- **Traceability Before Inference**: Every finding is backed by verbatim source snippets and verified page numbers. Unverifiable citations are explicitly flagged.
- **Auditable Accountability**: Every status transition records the reviewer identity, timestamp, and mandatory clinical rationale.
